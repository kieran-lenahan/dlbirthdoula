About

Hey there! I'm Dana.

I am a wife. I am a mom. And I am a passionate, trained birth doula.

My doula journey

After my own positive birth experience, I was flooded with memories of how many women shared their stories of trials, disappointments, and even trauma from their birth experiences. The fire was ignited.

I cannot express the righteous indignation I feel whenever I hear of a woman whose feelings were ignored, whose natural process was interrupted, or who was scared into a decision without all of the information to make an informed choice — if a choice was even presented. It is because of these stories that birth has become, to so many people, something to be feared.

But I believe that growing and bringing a life into this world is a beautiful, messy, and sacred process that connects women to their God-given power. Whether you give birth at home or in the hospital, vaginally or via cesarean, with medication or without — every single woman deserves to feel empowered, loved, and in control.

That comes through education, information, and support of your motherly intuition. It comes from feeling safe, feeling complete trust, and a willingness to be vulnerable.

Training & background

I attended my DONA approved doula training program in 2021.

I hold a Bachelor of Science in speech-language pathology and audiology with a minor in psychology, and a Master of Arts in speech-language pathology. I practiced as a licensed speech therapist in the medical setting for four years before turning to birth work full-time.

A few things about me

I consider myself a lifelong athlete — I was a competitive swimmer for 15 years and swam for a Division I team in college. I love being outside: near water, in the mountains, or on a nature walk at the park.

On rainy days we get the rain gear on and take the kids out to play in puddles. There is no such thing as bad weather, just bad clothing. Then, of course, soup and homemade bread.

My self care is what I call "mandatory feet up time" — 15 minutes, a small afternoon coffee, feet on the couch. It has been life changing.

Services

Continuous support, from the first question to the fourth trimester.

BeforePreparing

Prenatal meetings

We will schedule two prenatal meetings where we discuss your birth plan, any fears you have, and how I — your doula — and your partner can best support you.

Virtual support

I am available by text, call, FaceTime, or Zoom. I like to stay informed after prenatal visits and I'm always around to answer questions and share resources as they come up.

This is true before your birth, and after.

DuringBirth support

Physical

I am trained to help you through:

  • Relaxation techniques
  • Hydrotherapy
  • Acupressure
  • Breath work
  • Physical assistance for coping in labor
  • Positions to help labor progress

Emotional

Pregnancy and birth can be an emotional rollercoaster. I am a trained birth professional with knowledge of the emotional changes that can happen within moments. I will coach you through decision making and empower YOU to make informed choices — supporting you, validating you, and encouraging you to trust your body and your baby.

Partner

Doulas are a source of support for expectant mothers AND partners. Partners, I am here to help you help her.

When you lean into the role of supporting a birthing momma, you will find a connection like no other. I am here to facilitate that connection.

Informational

Pregnancy and childbirth come with so many unknowns. While I am not a crystal ball, I can provide information and resources for your specific, non-medical questions. I am also a great resource for learning HOW to best communicate with care providers, friends, and family.

AfterSettling in

Documentation

I am happy to capture moments in all stages of birth through pictures and videos. I will always prioritize mom over photos, but I do my best to capture the beauty of the process when a birth photographer isn't present.

I also provide a written outline of your labor and birth experience as a keepsake.

Postpartum

After your life-changing, incredible, miraculous experience of birthing a human, we will schedule two meetings. One visit is required, ideally 1–2 weeks after birth; the other, if you'd like it, comes 4–6 weeks after.

We'll follow up on your birth experience, check on how you are adjusting to adding another human to your day-to-day life, and answer any postpartum questions.

Doula facts

There is nothing to lose in hiring a doula, and so much to gain.

The largest systematic review of continuous labor support analyzed 21 randomized control studies covering 15,000 women. The findings are evidence based, with no known risks. According to the summary in the DONA Birth Position Paper, doula-supported women were:

28%less likely to need a cesarean section
31%less likely to use pitocin to speed up labor
9%less likely to use any pain medication
34%less likely to rate their birth negatively

What do these facts mean for you? A doula-supported birth is not only for those who want an unmedicated birth. Hiring a birth doula is most beneficial for preventing avoidable interventions when mom and baby are both healthy — and having a doula present has the highest improvement in positive childbirth experiences, no matter how mom chooses to proceed.

A doula never replaces your partner. Continuous support simply means you are never left alone with only hospital staff. See services above for how I support partners and facilitate their involvement.
